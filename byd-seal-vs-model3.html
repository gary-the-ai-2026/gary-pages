The Balanced View
What Owners Actually Say
BYD Seal Premium — wins where it counts for a family
More car for the money. Significantly more power (230 vs 208 kW), near-identical or better range (570 km) from a much bigger battery (82.56 vs 62.5 kWh), leather interior, proper instrument cluster and HUD.

A proper dashboard. Owners repeatedly cite this over the Tesla — "an instrument cluster in front of the driver," a normal, comfortable, old-school-but-modern cabin.

LFP Blade battery you can charge to 100% daily without degrading it, plus a longer 6-year vehicle warranty than the base Tesla's 5.

Community read: "Seal was the clear winner… every finish feels more premium… and it has a dashboard."
Tesla Model 3 RWD — wins where it compounds over years
Brand new, for less money. $50,544 drive-away vs $51,223 for a 5,580 km demo. First owner, 0 km, full factory warranty from day one.

Supercharger network + 250 kW charging. Genuine long-distance touring capability and the best DC charging in the country — worth real money for road trips.

Software that improves forever. Regular OTA updates that add features and safety over the car's life; the strongest driver-assist suite in the segment.

Better future resale path — Tesla holds brand recognition and a proven resale market; the base RWD is now under-cutting the Seal's value pitch.

Community read: "better charging ecosystem, proven drivetrain, excellent software and constant updates."
Both experts and owners land on the same verdict: the Seal Premium is the better purchase for most buyers (more car, more power, more battery, nicer cabin, longer warranty at the same money); the Model 3 is the better long-term ownership proposition (new, better charging network, relentless software, better resale). The decision isn't which is a better car — it's whether you'd rather have the richer car today or the better long-term ownership story.

The Call
Gary's View

Now a genuine coin-flip — lean Seal for power & cabin, Tesla for newness & range

My first pass flattered the Seal. The real data narrows this dramatically — the brand-new Tesla claims more WLTP range than your demo Seal (572 vs 570 km), and costs $679 less.
  1. The Seal is no longer the obvious value win. Doing a demo premium sedan against a brand-new entry sedan at near-identical money, the Seal keeps only these real edges: more power (230 vs 208 kW), a much bigger battery (82.5 vs 62.5 kWh), a leather/HUD cabin, and 1 extra year of warranty (6 vs 5). Everything else is a wash or favours Tesla.
  2. The Tesla's case is now strong: brand new, 0 km, equal-or-better range, Supercharger network, 250 kW charging, OTA software, V2L/Powershare, and the strongest resale path in the segment — all for less money than your Seal deposit deal.
  3. What you'd give up in the base Tesla vs the Premium/SWV: ventilated front seats, the rear touchscreen, heated rear seats, full-width ambient lighting, richer upholstery (textile not full leatherette), 7 vs 9 speakers, and the Premium's frequency-selective dampers (base gets conventional). In an Adelaide January, the missing seat ventilation is legitimately felt.
  4. The deposit is the tie-breaker. You've put $500 down on the Seal (08/10). If that's refundable, the Tesla genuinely deserves equal odds. If it's forfeit, add it to the Seal column before you flip.
If you lean Tesla, verify the exact drive-away on your order link — $50,544 is indicative SA data, not a quote. Ask me to pull the live configurator total before signing. Range claims are WLTP; both are ~15–20% lower in real-world use.

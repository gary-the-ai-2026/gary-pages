The Questions You've Asked

These aren't hypotheticals. These are the questions every solo operator asks themselves — usually when they should be sleeping. Let's answer each one.

Question 1
"What happens to my leads at 2am?"
Right now? They sit in your inbox. A tradie fills in your form at 11pm after a long day on the tools. By 2am, they've had a reply from a competitor who uses automated acknowledgment. By 8am, when you open your laptop, the lead has already formed a first impression — of someone who responded faster than you.
The Answer
Your sales AI agent doesn't sleep. When that 2am lead comes in, it's acknowledged within minutes — not with a generic auto-reply, but with a personalised message that qualifies their needs and suggests next steps. The lead wakes up to a response. You wake up to a qualified lead summary.
Question 2
"Who follows up on holidays?"
Nobody, currently. And you wouldn't expect anyone to — it's a holiday. You're with family. But leads don't stop arriving just because it's a public holiday. In fact, tradies often use holidays to research new services. A lead that arrives on Christmas Day is a lead that's serious enough to be thinking about their business on Christmas Day.
The Answer
Your AI team doesn't take holidays. It doesn't take weekends. It doesn't take sick days. Every lead, every inquiry, every follow-up — 365 days a year. You enjoy the holiday. The business doesn't pause.
"What about the leads that go cold after three days?"
They're not bad leads. They're leads that weren't nurtured. A three-day gap in communication drops close probability by 30% or more. By day seven, the lead has mentally moved on — not because they chose someone else, but because the momentum died. You were busy closing the deal in front of you. The one behind you slipped away.
The Answer
Your sales agent runs a structured follow-up cadence: day 1 thank-you, day 3 value-add, day 7 check-in, day 14 final nudge. Each message is personalised based on the original conversation. You approve before send. No lead goes cold because nobody remembered to follow up.
"How much am I actually losing?"
You feel it more than you can quantify it. But here's the math: at 3–5 missed jobs per month and an $8k average deal, that's $24k–$40k in revenue that was yours to win — if you'd had the capacity to reach it. Over a year, that's $288k–$480k. Not lost to a better closer. Lost to an empty inbox at 2am, a follow-up that never went out, a lead that went cold.
The Answer
$24k–$40k/month in recoverable revenue. Your AI team costs ~$70/month. The ROI isn't a percentage — it's a completely different category. Recover one deal and the system pays for itself for years.
"Is AI actually ready for a real business?"
This is the one that stops most people. The fear that AI is a toy — fine for generating images or writing emails, but not trustworthy enough for real business operations. It's a fair question. And the answer depends entirely on how it's set up.
The Answer
This isn't a chatbot you open in a browser. It's a team of specialist agents running on production infrastructure — the same agent framework (Hermes) and language model (DeepSeek) that have been running 24/7 in production for 4+ months across 18 platforms. It operates with a human-in-the-loop approval model: agents act, you review, agents learn. Mistakes happen once. The system gets smarter every week.
"What if I just hired someone instead?"
A part-time admin person costs $25–$35/hour. At 20 hours/week, that's $2,000–$2,800/month. They work business hours. They take holidays. They get sick. They make mistakes. They need training. And when they leave, they take the process knowledge with them.
The Answer
Your AI team costs ~$70/month total. Works 24/7/365. Documents every action. Learns from every correction. Doesn't quit. And the processes are encoded in skills — not in someone's head. If you scale, the AI team scales. No hiring, no onboarding, no management overhead.

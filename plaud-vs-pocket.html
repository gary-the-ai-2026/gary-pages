Plaud AI vs Pocket AI

Two pocket-sized devices that record, transcribe and summarise real-world conversations. Which one fits your stack?

Our Pick

Pocket — for an AI-native stack

~A$150
US$99 intro (reg. US$199) · free tier transcribes for life
  • You pick the AI model behind every summary (Claude, GPT, etc.) — Plaud locks you to its own pipeline
  • MCP server since day one — transcripts queryable from Claude Code
  • Cheapest to run: unlimited transcription on the free tier, no forced subscription
The safer turnkey pick is Plaud Note Pro. It's more established, has real Australian distribution (au.plaud.ai + Kogan), stronger compliance (SOC 2, HIPAA) and better battery. Pocket ships from Hong Kong and is the newer product. Choose Plaud if you want zero-fuss — choose Pocket if you already live in Claude and want control over the AI doing the work.

Both devices produce usable transcripts on clean one-on-one audio. Every source — including the affiliate reviews — agrees the raw audio is at parity. The gap is the layer above: which AI writes the summary, and how fast you can retrieve it. That's where Pocket's model choice and MCP shine, and where Plaud's fixed pipeline shows its ceiling.
The Call
Which one should you buy?

Buy Pocket if…

the AI-native option
  • You already run Claude Code, MCP, or an agentic workflow
  • You want a vote in which model writes your summaries
  • You want the cheapest year-one cost and unlimited transcription
  • You prefer a clip-on wearable to a card

Buy Plaud Note Pro if…

the turnkey option
  • You want the established device with a deep review history
  • You need real AU distribution (direct store, Kogan) and fast local shipping
  • Compliance matters — SOC 2, HIPAA, ISO for work/health use
  • Phone-call capture is your main use case (magnetic attachment)

A note on recording consent

Recording conversations is legal in most Australian jurisdictions with one-party consent, but the safe habit is to disclose recording up front — every meeting, regardless of jurisdiction. Both devices make the act invisible to the other party, which is precisely why the disclosure matters.

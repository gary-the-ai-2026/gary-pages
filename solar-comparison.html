Why the battery matters more than the panels: Solar generates during cheap solar sponge hours (16.8c/kWh). The battery shifts that energy to the peak window (54c/kWh) — a 3.2× value multiplier. Without a battery, you'd export at 5c and import at 54c. The spread is the entire business case.

NRG's 36kWh edge: On a 40°C summer day with pool + AC + sauna, evening draw could hit 10–12 kWh. Glow's 32kWh battery (~29kWh usable) would be depleted by morning. NRG's 36kWh gives a critical buffer. But in winter, both systems have ample capacity.
The Call
Recommendation UPDATED 14 JUL
Our Pick — Confirmed with Actual Data

Glow Option 2 — $19,460

5.4yr payback · $19,491 net by Year 10

The actual Amber rates and offset cost modelling confirm Glow as the right choice. Here's why:

  1. Faster offset recovery. Glow repays the offset account in Year 6. NRG takes until Year 7. The $7,760 you save upfront stays in offset earning 6.04% from day one — that's $469/yr in avoided interest just from choosing the cheaper system.
  2. Actual rates improved the case. The blended rate came in at 33.6c (vs 30c assumed), boosting self-consumption savings by 12%. The peak window is a predictable 54c — not the volatile 40–60c range we feared. Both changes favour the system you can get sooner.
  3. 20kW inverter is future-proof. Lincoln's 3-phase upgrade eliminates the bottleneck concern. Glow's 20kW inverter has more headroom than NRG's 15kW for pool + sauna + future EV.
  4. Local installer, established support. Glow has operated in SA since 1997 with a 10-year workmanship warranty. GoodWe has mature Australian support. NRG's Sigenergy is technically superior but newer to market.
  5. At 10 years, they tie. The offset cost equalises the financial outcome. NRG doesn't pull meaningfully ahead until Year 13+. If you're still at Mitcham in 2040, NRG would have been the better long-term bet — but that's a long time to wait for a payoff.

What you give up vs NRG:

  • 5.1 kW less solar (14.25 vs 19.38 kW)
  • 4 kWh less battery (32 vs 36 kWh) — marginal in winter, noticeable in peak summer
  • Dual-glass panels — Jinko's standard backsheet is fine for Adelaide but Trina's dual-glass is objectively better
  • Native Amber SmartShift — GoodWe needs Home Assistant/ChargeHQ for dynamic dispatch
  • Modular battery expansion — GoodWe is a fixed stack
  • Built-in blackout protection and EV charger readiness

If you want the premium option: NRG's Sigenergy ecosystem is genuinely better engineered — dual-glass panels, modular battery, native Amber integration, EV-ready. If this is your 20+ year forever home and you value future-proofing over near-term cash flow, NRG is defensible. But it costs $7,760 more today and doesn't pull ahead financially until Year 13.

Bottom line: Glow at $19,460 is excellent value from a local installer with a 20kW inverter, 32kWh battery, and solid Jinko panels. The offset cost analysis removes NRG's financial edge — they tie at Year 10. Glow is the lower-risk choice with faster payback. Go with Glow.

Reading This Panel

October 2026 is the first panel since starting Mounjaro; June 2026 was the pre-treatment baseline. These changes therefore bundle medication, weight loss and ordinary biological variation together and cannot be attributed to any one of them. The liver picture is genuinely improving — ALT back to its February level, GGT down 23% — but ALP is unmoved at 223, and two new developments (platelets 113→100, ferritin 57→152) belong in front of Prof Coates rather than being read as noise. Note also that June was run by Clinicalabs and October by ClinPath: ferritin assays are not standardised between laboratories, so that jump is not a clean like-for-like comparison.

Platelets

9 draws: Mar 2022 → Oct 2026. Reference: 150–450 ×10⁹/L. Declined to a 2026 nadir, now oscillating in a narrow band.

Platelet Commentary

Platelets declined steadily from 143 (Mar 2022) to a nadir of 98 (Feb 2026), then recovered to 113 in June and slipped back to 100 in October. Across the last five draws the count has sat in a narrow 98–114 band with no downward drift — a stable mild thrombocytopenia rather than a progressive one. The Oct 2026 report reads "red cells appear normal. White cell morphology appears normal. Large platelets are present. CONCLUSION: Mild thrombocytopenia persists." Large platelets indicate the bone marrow is actively compensating. Reports from Sep 2025 to Jun 2026 have variously attributed the finding to drugs, immune causes, viral infection and liver disease; no single cause has been established.

Liver Panel
Liver Enzymes — 5 Timepoints

ALT · AST · GGT · Alkaline Phosphatase

Nov 2024 → Oct 2026. All four remain above range, but ALT and GGT have fallen sharply from their June peaks.

Liver Commentary

June 2026 was the worst panel on record — ALT 112 (highest ever) and GGT 155 — but October reverses most of that: ALT down 39 points to 73 (back to its February level), GGT down 36 points to 119, AST effectively flat at 64. The stubborn marker is Alkaline Phosphatase, unchanged at 223 (2.1× ULN) across Feb, Jun and Oct. ALP has both liver and bone sources: when GGT falls but ALP holds steady, the biliary tree becomes a less likely explanation and bone turnover more likely — and bone resorption rises with weight loss. That distinction is worth a bone-specific ALP or isoenzyme test. History: the Nov 2024 request carried the clinical note "TRANSPLANT PRE OP ABNORMAL LFT", so liver function has been under surveillance since at least mid-2024.

Urinalysis
Urine — Albumin/Creatinine Ratio

Urine Albumin/Creatinine Ratio

Random urine. Reference: 0.0–3.5 mg/mmol. Nov 2024 and Oct 2026 were both flagged HIGH; Jun 2026 urine protein sat below the assay range so no ratio was calculable.

Urine Commentary

November 2024 recorded an elevated ratio of 5.4 mg/mmol (albumin 31.1 mg/L) which had resolved by June 2026. October 2026 records 6.9 mg/mmol — flagged HIGH, but with an important caveat: the absolute albumin was only 18.0 mg/L (under the <20 cut-off) and the urine creatinine was just 2.6 mmol/L, i.e. a dilute sample. Dividing a normal albumin by a low creatinine is what pushes the ratio over the line. The laboratory comment reads: "An increased albumin/creatinine ratio is noted, but not to a pathologically significant level. Progress renal function and microalbumin is suggested in 6 months." Repeat in ~6 months; a first-morning sample would be more reliable.
